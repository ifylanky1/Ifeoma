### Structure
1. [HOOK] (about 250 words): Open with "Tonight" or a direct address. Use a run of negations that flips into affirmations ("You are not… You are not… Tonight you are covered."). Name the Blood of Jesus within 3 sentences. List the stakes: life, home, sleep, family, destiny. Add "The same God who…" callbacks to Bible victories, and end with a line of menace toward the enemy.
2. [THE NIGHTLY WATCH] (about 450 words): Ask for a 7, then 14, then 30-night habit and explain why consistency matters. Describe outcomes modestly ("many who pray this way begin to notice…"). Tell the chosen Bible story vividly as the testimony and tie it to tonight.
3. [THE WEAPON] (about 450 words): A delayed reveal ("There is one weapon the enemy cannot stand against…"), teaching from 2–3 scriptures, then the first spoken first-person declaration ("Say this with me, out loud, right where you are lying…").
4. [THE COVERING] (about 450 words): Intercession over "this child of God" and "this household," then a first-person declaration.
5. [BRIDGE] (about 120 words): "Now we walk the battlefield…" Tell the listener to speak every line from here as their own.
6. [WAVE 1] through [WAVE 12–15] (about 300–400 words each), one target per wave, each following this template:
   a. A thesis strike: "I [strong verb] every [target]… and I [verb] every [related target]."
   b. A concrete list of 3–6 specific items.
   c. The mechanism: the blood seals, the fire consumes, the angels pursue, the light exposes.
   d. A scripture anchor: "It is written in [Book chapter and verse]. [exact KJV wording]."
   e. A weaponization line, varied each wave ("I take this word as a sword…", "I lift this word like a banner…").
   f. An enforcement burst of 4–8 short commands.
   g. A closing seal line, then a hard cut to the next wave.
7. [MID-PRAYER CALL] (about 80 words), after about 45% of the script: "Type '[declaration]' in the comments" plus "share this with one person who needs it tonight."
8. [THE FINAL WORD] (about 550 words): A roll-call of verdict fragments with the verb dropped ("Every door, sealed. Every altar, burned. Every arrow, reversed."), then a rapid run of 6–8 scriptures with a one-line application each, then a closing verdict and a second comment call-to-action.
9. [THE QUIET HOUSE] (about 450 words): The cooldown for sleep. The fire becomes warmth; the sentences slow down; guide the breathing; "you do not have to keep watch, he does"; 1–2 rest scriptures (Psalm 4:8, Psalm 127:2, Proverbs 3:24).
10. [THE SEAL] (about 200 words): A soft first-person declaration, then a sealing prayer, ending "In the mighty name of Jesus Christ… Amen."

### Voice and rhythm
- Decree and declare; never ask or hope. No "if it be your will," "maybe" or "try to."
- Base sentence: "I + strong verb + object," 6–14 words. Verbs: seal, bind, burn, break, cancel, revoke, reverse, sever, expose, scatter, uproot, silence, cover, decree.
- Use anaphora in runs of 3–5 ("Every… Every… Every…").
- Rhythm: build, build, build, then punch. After one long catalogue sentence, write three 2–4 word sentences.
- Use contrast pairs ("not X, but Y") and triple-adjective closers ("living, burning, and unbreakable").
- Cycle the address modes: "you" to teach, "we" and "this household" to intercede, "I" and "my" to declare.
- Start sections with momentum words: Now, Tonight, Right now.
- Repeat the chorus scripture 4–6 times.
- Intensity curve: fierce for about 75% of the script, then a calm landing for sleep.
- Write for the ear: no parentheses, no lists in the spoken text, no stage directions.

- Never invent a verse or present a paraphrase as a quotation.
